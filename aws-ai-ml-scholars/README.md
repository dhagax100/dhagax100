# AWS AI & ML Scholars — Journey Guide

A living companion for Nuradin's acceptance into the **AWS AI & ML Scholars
"Future AWS AI Programmer" Nanodegree** (Udacity, sponsored by AWS). This
folder exists to answer "what do I do next?" at every stage, since the
program is new to you.

## ⚠️ First: verify before you click anything

The acceptance email has a couple of details worth double-checking before
acting on it:

- The program name ("Future AWS AI Programmer Nanodegree") doesn't match
  Udacity's publicly documented "AWS AI & ML Scholarship Program" naming —
  it may just be a 2026 rebrand, but confirm.
- The email body has a rendering artifact ("before the session.above.") that
  is a common tell in phishing templates, not necessarily proof of anything,
  but a reason for caution.

**Before registering for the kickoff or downloading anything:**
1. Log in to udacity.com directly (don't use the email's links) and check
   your account/dashboard for the same enrollment.
2. Check for a matching notice in your AWS account (if one was used to
   apply) or the AWS Skill Builder / re/Start portal.
3. Only then use the official links to register for the Zoom kickoff.

Once confirmed legitimate, everything below applies.

## How this guide helps

Udacity scholarship programs are typically structured in phases: a
foundations/prerequisite course everyone takes, followed by a full
Nanodegree track for the students who complete it, with community support
(Slack/Discord), mentors, and office hours throughout. Since the exact
syllabus for this cohort isn't public yet, treat the checklist in
[`progress-tracker.md`](./progress-tracker.md) as a starting skeleton —
update it with the real module names once you see your Udacity classroom.

Use this folder as the place to:
- Track which phase/module you're in and what's due next.
- Keep notes on anything confusing so you can ask a mentor or office hours.
- Log AWS services/concepts as you learn them, for quick review later.

Ask me (Claude) to update `progress-tracker.md` whenever your real syllabus,
deadlines, or project requirements come in — paste the classroom content or
emails and I'll turn them into checklist items and explain anything unclear.
