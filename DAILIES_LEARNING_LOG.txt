CURRENT RULE SET (as of 2026-09-28)
------------------------------------

BIAS
- Weekly/Daily structure sets directional control (BUY/SELL/NONE).
- 4H/1H POIs are only tradeable in the direction Weekly/Daily permits.

SESSIONS / TRADING WINDOW
- Real trading window: 10:00-20:00 Riyadh in summer, 11:00-21:00 in
  winter (London open through New York close). DST-detected from real
  New York transition dates, not a hardcoded month range.
- Asia is liquidity-grab only — never an entry session.
- Nothing before the window opens or after it closes counts: no setup,
  no entry, no display.

POI ELIGIBILITY
- A POI only counts if some real lifecycle event (trigger, eligible,
  impact, or breach) happens inside the window that day.
- "Old" POIs (OOB/ORB/OFVG) never show or trade on H4/1H, period —
  even though they're a real tradeable category at Daily/Weekly.
- PREMIUM/DISCOUNT gate: the 50/50 split between the zone's
  protect_level and the most recent opposing-kind swing confirmed
  before impact. Price must have reached that midpoint before impact
  counts as a real reaction, not a random touch.

ENTRY (the real, ported rule — not the earlier simplified version)
- After impact + premium/discount confirmed: find the first confirmed
  5m swing of the SL-anchor kind ("resting" swing). That starts the
  search.
- The entry level ("candidate") is the most recent opposite-kind swing
  at/before the resting swing. It can be REPLACED by a newer swing that
  confirms before price actually breaks it — the stop order chases the
  newest swing, it's a race, not a fixed level.
- Entry fires the moment price breaks the (possibly-updated) candidate
  level.

STOP LOSS
- SL = the EXTREME (not just the most recent) of every qualifying
  SL-anchor swing between impact and the entry minute.

TAKE PROFIT
- Fixed 3R. Always.

POI VIOLATION / INVALIDATION (the gap that got fixed 2026-09-28)
- A POI dies, in real time, the instant EITHER of these happens —
  checked on every 1-minute bar, before the entry-break test:
    (a) a completed 4H/1H candle closes its BODY at or beyond the near
        edge of the zone (a bare wick doesn't count, but a close INSIDE
        the zone counts, not just fully through it).
    (b) the zone's own protect_level (its protecting swing, snapshotted
        at creation) gets exceeded, wick or not, at the exact minute it
        happens.
- Whichever happens first kills the setup. A resting swing or entry
  candidate that would only form AFTER that moment never gets a trade —
  it's a dead setup, not a missed one.

RE-ENTRY (second entry rule)
- After a plain stop-loss exit (not TP, not still open, not a breach),
  re-arm and search again from the exact SL exit time — as long as the
  POI hasn't been structurally invalidated by then.
- The chain stops the moment an attempt resolves to anything other than
  a plain SL: a win, an open trade, or the POI dying first.
- A re-entry attempt (2nd+) that never actually became a trade is not
  reported anywhere — no row, no box, nothing. It's still used
  internally to know the chain is over, but it's not "a missed
  opportunity" worth showing.
- Open question, not yet decided for Dailies: the old OB/RB/FVG project
  found "2nd entry of a 3+-attempt POI" had a 0% win rate on that
  dataset (-12R across 12 trades) — a real reason to consider capping
  attempts. Not applied here yet; flagged for a decision.

DEDUP
- Two POIs producing the identical entry (same price, same trigger
  minute) collapse into ONE trade. One real move, not one trade per POI
  that happened to touch it.

2026-09-28 — First full case study: 12 January 2026, real data
- Bias: SELL. Window: 11:00-21:00 Riyadh (winter).
- 7 POIs alive in the window. Only 2 (OB#44 + RB#67, same
  protect_level, impacted a minute apart) produced a trade — deduped
  into one: SELL entry 14:32 @ 1.16847, SL 1.16892, TP 1.16712.
- REAL OUTCOME: stopped out at 16:08. A loss, -1R (4.5 pips risked).
  Confirmed by simulating forward through the actual M1 data to the
  real exit, not assumed.
- The POI was structurally invalidated (protect_level broken) at the
  SAME minute the SL was hit — 16:08. Because of that, the re-entry
  chain correctly refused to re-arm: the setup was already dead the
  instant it lost. This is the invalidation gate doing real work on
  real data, not just theory.
- Why the other 5 POIs never traded:
  - RB#68 (SELL, impacted 16:07) — invalidated at 16:08, one minute
    later. Never had time to form a resting swing.
  - RB#69 (BUY, impacted 17:25) — invalidated at 17:29, same pattern.
  - FVG#9 and FVG#31 (both BUY) — never reached the premium/discount
    midpoint before their own impact. Not a bias-filter exclusion —
    these failed on their own structural terms.
  - OB#45 (BUY) — never impacted at all.
- Lesson: most POIs in a window die fast. Impact alone means nothing —
  the premium check and the invalidation clock start racing
  immediately, and most setups lose that race before a trade is even
  possible. A POI whose protect_level sits very close to its own likely
  entry/SL should be treated as fragile going in, not as strong
  confirmation just because it impacted.

2026-09-28 — Ported the real entry/SL/re-entry/invalidation rules
- Learned (from the previously-built and dataset-validated
  five_bso_engine.py, SPEC.md SS17/20-27) that the Dailies 5m trade
  logic had been a simplified approximation: fixed first-swing entry,
  last-swing SL, and — critically — no check at all for whether the POI
  had been violated (body close or protect_level break) between impact
  and entry. The user asked directly whether this was checked; it
  wasn't. Ported the real rules (see rule set above) and verified them
  end-to-end against the real EURUSD M1 dataset for 12 Jan 2026.

2026-09-28 — Fixed two real bugs found on the live 5m chart

  missing xloc=xloc.bar_time, so Pine read the epoch-millisecond
  x-coordinates as an absurd future bar index.
- An unrequested BUY trade appeared on a day scoped to SELL only:
  compute_5m_trades() computed both directions unconditionally, ignoring
  --default-side. Added a side parameter so it matches the rest of the
  indicator.
