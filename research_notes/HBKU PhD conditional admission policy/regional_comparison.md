# Regional Comparison: Applying Before GRE/IELTS/TOEFL Scores Are In Hand (Gulf PhD Admissions)

**Scope note:** WebFetch to hbku.edu.qa and www.hbku.edu.qa was blocked in this research session by the sandbox's network allowlist (DNS resolution failure for both hostnames), so all HBKU-specific findings below come from WebSearch result summaries that quote/paraphrase the official pages, not from directly fetched raw HTML. These summaries appear to quote exact text from the pages (marked as such below), but could not be independently re-verified by direct fetch in this session. Treat HBKU quotes as "search-engine-mediated primary source" rather than fully verified primary source, and flag this to the applicant.

## Key Question 1: Across HBKU's other PhD programs, is the stated policy on WHEN test scores are due consistent, or do some programs explicitly allow later submission while others don't?

### Takeaway
HBKU's own program pages are inconsistent and in places self-contradictory about whether GRE/GMAT is even required, and none of the pages found state that scores may be submitted *after* the application deadline or after a decision — but several use softer "optional" or "where applicable" language for some programs (notably Computer Science and Engineering) that is harder-edged ("at the time of application") for others (Sustainable Environment/Energy). No HBKU page found explicitly says "you may submit scores later" for a PhD program; the "as part of the online application... official copies by the deadline" framing for Sustainable Energy is NOT contradicted elsewhere — if anything, the Sustainable Environment PhD page is even stricter in wording.

### Cited Findings
- PhD in Computer Science and Engineering (CSE): the program factsheet reportedly states "Submitting a valid GRE or GMAT test score at the time of application is optional for the PhD program in Computer Science and Engineering program," and an Arabic-language version of the CSE admission-requirements page marks the PhD's GRE/GMAT as "Optional." — [CSE PhD factsheet](https://hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/program-files/cse-phd-computer-science-engineering-factsheet.pdf); [CSE admission requirements](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/cse/admission-requirements)
- PhD/MS in Sustainable Environment: the factsheet reportedly states "Applicants to the Master of Science and PhD Programs in Sustainable Environment are required to submit a GRE General test score **at the time of application**." The PhD program page itself is described as more hedged, saying GRE is required "where applicable" under CSE admissions requirements. — [Sustainable Environment MS factsheet](https://hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/program-files/cse-ms-sustainable-environment-factsheet.pdf); [PhD in Sustainable Environment](https://hbku.edu.qa/en/node/4123)
- A university-wide HBKU graduate admission policy PDF reportedly states standardized test scores (citing GRE/GMAT as examples) "are required for all PhD programs offered in English" — but this document's revision history reportedly dates back to 2008, raising doubt about whether it reflects current practice. — [HBKU graduate admission policy PDF](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/ph/graduate_admission.pdf)
- No HBKU PhD program page found (CSE, Sustainable Environment/Energy, Islamic Studies, Public Policy) contains explicit language permitting submission of GRE/IELTS/TOEFL scores after the application deadline or after a conditional offer. Where timing is specified at all, it is "at the time of application" or "with the online application," consistent with the Sustainable Energy page's framing the applicant is already working from.
- College of Islamic Studies (CIS) PhD: the official PhD program page reportedly lists IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL 79 and refers applicants to admissions.hbku.edu.qa "for exemption details," while the CIS college-wide admission-requirements page (covering multiple programs, unclear if PhD included) gives a much lower threshold (TOEFL 35–45 or IELTS 5) with exemption possible for applicants who studied in English — a direct numeric inconsistency across HBKU's own pages for the same college. — [PhD in Islamic Studies](https://hbku.edu.qa/en/node/29715); [CIS admission requirements](https://hbku.edu.qa/cis-admission-requirements)
- CIS PhD also requires a separate Arabic-language proficiency test administered by the College "at the time of application" for all applicants (native and non-native Arabic speakers), with exemption for those who completed university studies in Arabic — illustrating that "at the time of application" language recurs as HBKU's standard phrasing for test requirements generally, not something unique to Sustainable Energy. — [PhD in Islamic Studies](https://hbku.edu.qa/en/node/29715)
- College of Public Policy (CPP): no PhD program was found advertised at all in search results — only Master of Public Policy, Master of Policy and Program Evaluation, a Graduate Certificate, and a Certificate in Public Policy, none of which mention a GRE requirement. The MPP lists IELTS 6.5/TOEFL 79. This suggests CPP may not currently run a PhD, or it was not indexed. — [CPP admission requirements](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/cpp/admission-requirements)
- No dedicated "PhD in Data Science" program page was found at HBKU; the closest matches are the MS in Data Science and Engineering and the PhD in Computer Science and Engineering. This means the comparison requested (PhD in Data Science specifically) could not be directly completed — see Gaps.
- Institutional test-submission codes are standardized across HBKU programs (GRE institution code 7551, TOEFL code 4981, GMAT code H6S, no IELTS code — IELTS centers send the Test Report Form directly to HBKU), suggesting a common centralized admissions test-processing system across colleges rather than fully independent per-program policies. — [CSE PhD factsheet](https://hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/program-files/cse-phd-computer-science-engineering-factsheet.pdf); multiple program factsheets

### Inferences
- The variation found (CSE PhD = GRE/GMAT "optional"; Sustainable Environment/Energy PhD = GRE "at the time of application," i.e., mandatory and time-bound) suggests HBKU's test-score policy is set at the program/college level, not uniformly by the university. This supports treating the Sustainable Energy program's own wording as controlling for that program, rather than assuming university-wide flexibility exists.
- The repeated, HBKU-wide phrase "at the time of application" (seen for Sustainable Environment's GRE and CIS's Arabic test) suggests this is HBKU's standard formal phrasing for "due with the application," not unique or especially strict wording chosen just for Sustainable Energy. It does not, by itself, prove zero tolerance — but it also provides no textual basis for assuming flexibility either.
- The internal inconsistency on CIS's English-score threshold (6.5/79 on one page vs. 35–45/5 on another) is evidence that HBKU's own published pages are not fully reliable or current, which cuts in two directions: it weakens confidence that any one HBKU page's exact wording is the final word, but it does not create evidence of schedule flexibility — just evidence of page-maintenance inconsistency.

### Gaps
- No PhD-specific page for "Data Science" was found; comparison requested in the brief could not be completed for that exact program.
- No current PhD program was confirmed for the College of Public Policy, so the requested CPP PhD comparison could not be completed.
- Could not directly fetch the raw HBKU pages (blocked by sandbox network policy) to verify wording beyond what WebSearch's summarization surfaced; there is some risk that search-result paraphrasing introduced small wording drift from the primary source.
- No HBKU FAQ, admissions-counselor Q&A, webinar transcript, YouTube video, or LinkedIn post explicitly addressing "I haven't taken the GRE/IELTS yet, can I still apply?" was found in this search. HBKU has held virtual "Graduate Studies Open House" events (confirmed via a Gulf Times article and an HBKU open-house page), but no transcript or recording surfaced with content on this specific question. This is a meaningful gap — it is the single most direct type of evidence the brief asked for, and none was found.

## Key Question 2: At comparable Gulf universities, what is the stated or commonly reported practice — apply-then-test, or test-then-apply?

### Takeaway
The clearest, most explicit "apply now, scores later" policy found among comparable Gulf universities is at **KAUST** (Saudi Arabia), which explicitly allows applicants without finished English or GRE scores to apply and supply a confirmed future test date instead, though the application cannot advance to faculty review without eventually submitting the score. Khalifa University (UAE) and Qatar University give more mixed signals — some pages require scores completed before/at application, others allow submission "by the deadline," and in one Khalifa University case an *undergraduate* FAQ (not graduate) explicitly allows late upload with processing paused until scores arrive. No clean "test-then-apply only, no exceptions" policy was found at any of the four comparator universities, but no unambiguous universal "apply-then-test is fine everywhere" policy was found either — the practice is program- and university-specific, and GRE requirements themselves vary (often optional) more than IELTS/TOEFL requirements do.

### Cited Findings
- **KAUST**: "Applicants who are unable to produce an English language proficiency test score or the optional GRE score by the deadline may still apply. However, you must submit your scores or upcoming test date including the confirmation/registration code to complete your application and move it forward to the faculty review committee." GRE is explicitly optional at KAUST ("KAUST does not require the GRE exam for admission"); English minimum is TOEFL 79 or IELTS 6.5, waived for degree-holders from US/Canada/UK/Ireland/Australia/New Zealand institutions. — [KAUST admission requirements](https://admissions.kaust.edu.sa/admission-requirment.html)
- **Khalifa University (UAE)**: Conflicting official-source signals. One KU FAQ states "All applicants must have completed TOEFL or IELTS before applying for a graduate program at Khalifa University. It is mandatory for all applicants to upload a copy of their English score certificate at the time of application." A different KU FAQ states the test must be completed "at the time they are applying... or by the deadline for submitting the applications, at the latest" — a looser standard. On GRE: the Graduate Catalog says a minimum GRE Quantitative score of 150 is required for admission to all Doctorate programs, but a more recent FAQ narrows the GRE requirement to only the "Direct PhD" route ("applicants who apply to the Direct PhD must have completed the GRE General Test at the time they are applying, or by the application deadline"). Current PhD postgraduate admissions page lists an overall IELTS of 6.5 plus research statement and interview, with Spring 2027 PhD applications open until 28 Sept 2026. — [KU Graduate Catalog](https://ku-ae.smartcatalogiq.com/en/2024-2025/graduate-catalog/graduate-admission-and-scholarships/required-qualifications/full-admission); [KU graduate admission FAQs](https://www.ku.ac.ae/graduate-admission-faqs); [KU postgraduate admission](https://www.ku.ac.ae/postgraduate-admission)
- Separately, Khalifa University's *undergraduate* (not graduate) FAQ explicitly states scores can be uploaded later, "but your application won't be processed until the scores are submitted" — a clear apply-then-test model, but for undergraduate admission, not PhD. This should NOT be read across to KU's PhD process, but is included as evidence that the "apply first, submit scores to unfreeze processing" model exists institutionally within at least one Gulf university's admissions machinery. — [Khalifa University graduate admissions required documents](https://www.ku.ac.ae/graduate-admissions-required-documents/)
- **Qatar University**: The QU Testing Center page states applicants to Diploma, Masters, and PhD programs "must submit competency test scores at application," with GRE sent electronically via ETS to QU's code (7569) and IELTS originals sent to QU Graduate Admissions — language suggesting scores are expected at the point of application, not after. The PhD in Business Administration page says GMAT is required (GRE may substitute in some cases) and the test must be taken within 2 years of the intended start semester (a validity rule, not directly a submission-timing rule). The PhD in Gulf Studies lists an official GRE score report among required documents without describing timing flexibility. — [QU Testing Center rules](https://cic.qu.edu.qa/en-us/testing-center/rules/Pages/graduates.aspx); [PhD in Gulf Studies](https://www.qu.edu.qa/en-us/colleges/artssciences/departments/international-affairs/phd-gulfstudies)
- **American University of Sharjah (AUS)**: No PhD program page found stating a GRE requirement (GMAT is confirmed required only for the PhD in Business Administration). AUS's own Doctoral English Language Requirement page accepts only TOEFL or IELTS (not IELTS Indicator, not TOEFL iBT Home Edition, not Linguaskill); no statement found on whether these must be submitted at application or can follow later. — [AUS Doctoral English Language Requirement](https://infodesk.aus.edu/hc/en-us/articles/25557292760732-Doctoral-English-Language-Requirement)

### Inferences
- Among the four named comparators, KAUST is the one clear, explicit, officially-stated "apply before scores are ready" policy for PhD-level admission — and notably, this is paired with GRE being fully optional there, so the flexibility is mainly about the English test, with an explicit "submit upcoming test date/confirmation code" mechanism.
- Khalifa University's internal inconsistency (one FAQ says "complete before applying," another says "by the deadline at the latest") suggests that even within a single Gulf university, publicly posted guidance on timing is not perfectly uniform — which is directly analogous to the kind of inconsistency found within HBKU's own pages (Key Question 1), and supports the general caution that a university's various public pages should not be assumed internally consistent or literally enforced to the letter.
- No comparator university was found with an explicit written policy stating GRE/IELTS must be *fully completed and scored before the application is even started* with zero flexibility (i.e., a hard "test-then-apply-only" policy with no exceptions documented anywhere) — but absence of a found statement is not proof such a policy doesn't exist at some of them (e.g., AUS, QU) in practice; it may simply not be published explicitly, which is itself notable.

### Gaps
- No stated AUS PhD-specific GRE policy was found at all (program pages did not surface in search; only the GMAT requirement for the Business PhD was confirmed), making it impossible to compare AUS's GRE timing practice specifically.
- Qatar University's timing policy for English test scores specifically (as opposed to GRE/GMAT) was not clearly found; the QU Testing Center page's "at application" language was the clearest but may not represent every QU PhD program's practice.
- None of the four comparators' pages were directly fetched in full (search-summarized only), so exact wording nuances may be lost; this is a general limitation across this entire research session due to the sandbox's network restriction on direct page fetches outside pre-approved domains (no such restriction applied to WebSearch, whose underlying fetcher appears unaffected).

## Key Question 3: Is there a general pattern in competitive, internationally-oriented Gulf PhD admissions of accepting "scores in progress" (test registered/scheduled but not yet sat) as part of an application?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented instance of "scores in progress" (a scheduled-but-not-yet-sat test) being explicitly accepted is KAUST's policy of accepting an "upcoming test date including the confirmation/registration code" in lieu of a completed score at the time of application. No other Gulf comparator in this research was found to explicitly describe accepting a "registered but not sat" test status; most either require the completed score "at" or "by" the application/deadline, or are silent on the question. This appears to be more an exception at specific universities than an established regional norm that can be generalized to HBKU.

### Cited Findings
- KAUST: explicitly accepts "your... upcoming test date including the confirmation/registration code" as a substitute for a completed score at the point of applying, pending eventual submission of the real score before the application proceeds to faculty review. — [KAUST admission requirements](https://admissions.kaust.edu.sa/admission-requirment.html)
- No equivalent "registered/scheduled test accepted as a placeholder" language was found for Khalifa University, Qatar University, AUS, or any HBKU program/page in this research session.
- General (non-Gulf-specific) US graduate-admissions sources describe a broader trend of GRE-optional and test-flexible policies spreading since the pandemic (e.g., Princeton's Graduate School leaves GRE minimums to individual departments and, from Fall 2026 admission, will only consider GRE scores if they remain valid as of the date the application opens — a validity rule, not a "scores in progress" acceptance rule). These are useful context for how test-timing flexibility is phrased elsewhere, but are not Gulf-specific and should not be read as describing Gulf practice. — [Princeton GRE requirements](https://gradschool.princeton.edu/gre-requirements-department)

### Inferences
- Because only one of the four named Gulf comparators (KAUST) has an explicit, discoverable "scores in progress accepted" policy, this research cannot support a claim that accepting scheduled-but-unsat tests is a general Gulf-regional norm. It is better characterized as a documented practice at one specific, unusually test-flexible institution (KAUST, which is also unusual in that it doesn't require the GRE at all) rather than a regional default that would predict how HBKU behaves.
- The absence of equivalent language at Khalifa University, Qatar University, or AUS does not prove those universities refuse "in progress" scores in practice — it may simply mean they haven't published guidance on the scenario, which is a different (and weaker) kind of evidence than an explicit refusal.

### Gaps
- No data was found (no forums, no university FAQ, no counselor statement) directly reporting actual applicant experience of being allowed or refused when applying with a merely-scheduled GRE/IELTS at any of the four comparators or at HBKU. This is a significant gap: the brief's core question — "does the university tolerate this in practice, regardless of the letter of the policy" — could not be answered with firsthand/anecdotal evidence from this search session. A search for Gulf-university applicant anecdotes ("I haven't taken the GRE/IELTS yet, can I still apply") returned no relevant forum threads, Reddit posts, or blog accounts; only official pages scoped to general requirements were found.

## Key Question 4: Does HBKU itself publish any FAQ, webinar transcript, or admissions-counselor statement addressing "I haven't taken the GRE/IELTS yet, can I still apply"?

### Takeaway
No such HBKU-specific FAQ, webinar transcript, YouTube recording, LinkedIn post, or admissions-counselor Q&A addressing this exact scenario was found in this research session. HBKU is confirmed to run periodic virtual "Graduate Studies Open House" events with Q&A breakout sessions, but no transcript, recording, or write-up of the content of those Q&A sessions was found — meaning this remains an open, unanswered question rather than a confirmed "no" or "yes."

### Cited Findings
- HBKU has held a "virtual graduate studies Open House" reported by Gulf Times, where the university's Office of Enrollment presented admission requirements and ran breakout Q&A sessions for prospective students; one instance was announced for a single evening session (6–8pm GMT+3), from a past admissions cycle. — [HBKU holds virtual graduate studies Open House — Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/705047/HBKU-holds-virtual-graduate-studies-Open-House-for); [HBKU Open House page](https://hbku.edu.qa/en/open-house)
- Separately, HBKU's own program pages repeatedly point applicants who lack qualifying English scores toward "admissions.hbku.edu.qa" for "exemption details" or "the process to seek exemption (if applicable)" — confirming that HBKU has *some* exception-handling process for the English-test requirement specifically, administered through the central admissions portal/office rather than documented in public FAQ text. No equivalent exemption-referral language was found for GRE timing specifically (as opposed to the test itself being waived).
- No YouTube videos, LinkedIn posts, or published open-house/webinar transcripts addressing test-score timing were found despite multiple targeted searches.

### Inferences
- The repeated redirection to "admissions.hbku.edu.qa for exemption details" across multiple HBKU program pages (CIS English test, others) suggests HBKU does handle individualized exceptions case-by-case through direct applicant-admissions-office contact rather than through self-service published policy — which is consistent with (though not proof of) a practice where applicants who contact admissions directly about an in-progress GRE/IELTS may receive case-by-case flexibility that isn't published anywhere for a general audience to rely on. This is an inference, not a confirmed fact.
- The fact that HBKU runs live Q&A open houses at all suggests that "can I apply before I have my scores" is very likely a commonly-asked question in those sessions (it is a universal applicant question across institutions), but this is speculation — no transcript confirms what was actually said.

### Gaps
- This is the single largest gap in the overall research: no direct evidence (quote, transcript, recording, or secondhand report) was found of HBKU staff explicitly answering "can I apply before taking GRE/IELTS?" The applicant's best path to closing this gap is to contact admissions.hbku.edu.qa directly and/or attend the next Graduate Studies Open House, rather than relying on inferred policy from program-page wording.
- No LinkedIn content from HBKU's admissions/enrollment staff was surfaced by search (searches returned only program pages); it's unclear whether this is because no such content exists, or because it wasn't indexed/surfaced by the search tool used.

## Overall Synthesis Note for Report Writer

- **HBKU-specific confidence level: LOW-TO-MODERATE that the Sustainable Energy PhD's "at the time of application... official by the deadline" wording is strictly enforced, and LOW confidence that it is flexible in practice** — because: (a) no HBKU source was found granting explicit flexibility for any PhD program (the opposite of what KAUST shows for itself), (b) the parallel Sustainable Environment program at HBKU uses language at least as strict, (c) but HBKU's own pages are demonstrably inconsistent with each other (CIS English-score thresholds, CSE's "optional" vs. the general policy PDF's "required for all PhD programs"), which means the literal wording on any single page may not be the final word in practice, and (d) HBKU does have *some* channel for individualized exceptions (the admissions.hbku.edu.qa referral pattern), though this is documented only for the English-test waiver scenario, not explicitly for timing.
- **Regional pattern: NOT uniform.** Of four named Gulf comparators, only KAUST has a clear, explicit "apply now, confirm your test date, submit scores before faculty review" policy — and KAUST is also unusual in making the GRE itself optional. Khalifa University and Qatar University lean toward "scores expected at or by the application deadline," with Khalifa University's own FAQs internally disagreeing on whether "before applying" or "by the deadline" is the real rule. AUS's practice could not be determined. This means the applicant should NOT assume "Gulf universities generally let you apply-then-test" as a safe general rule — it is institution-specific, and the one clear example of explicit flexibility (KAUST) differs from HBKU's own published framing.
- **Most actionable recommendation implied by this research (not itself a finding, but a synthesis for the writer to consider including):** Given the absence of any HBKU-specific statement granting flexibility, and the explicit "at the time of application" wording recurring in HBKU's own comparable program (Sustainable Environment), the applicant should treat HBKU's stated deadline-bound requirement as the operative rule unless and until admissions.hbku.edu.qa confirms otherwise directly, rather than relying on an assumed regional norm — since the regional norm itself is not uniform and the one clear counterexample (KAUST) is explicitly different from, not a proxy for, HBKU's policy.
