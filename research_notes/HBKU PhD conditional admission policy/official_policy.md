## What does HBKU's current Graduate Admission Policy say verbatim about conditional admission, and does a missing GRE/English score count as a valid condition?

### Takeaway
HBKU's graduate admission policy (AC-G-01, the only version found is V03, endorsed by the Provost on 6 April 2017 — no newer dated version was found) permits conditional admission generally, but a separate "University Conditional Admission Committee" document is the more specific source: it explicitly lists "not attaining the required score on a standardized test" as a reviewable condition, but states conditional admission for PhD applicants is normally for master's-level applicants and is only considered for PhD "under exceptional circumstances." No HBKU source found names a missing IELTS/TOEFL (English proficiency) score specifically as a PhD conditional-admission trigger — English-proficiency exemptions appear to be handled through a separate "exemption" process, not through the conditional-admission committee.

### Cited Findings
- HBKU's graduate admission policy PDF (titled "graduate_admission.pdf", version V03, endorsed by the Provost on 6 April 2017 — this is the only version surfaced; a 2024/2025-dated revision was searched for and not found) states: "Programs may admit students on a conditional basis," "The conditions must be clearly articulated in the admission letter," and "All conditional admission cases must receive pre-approval from the Provost." — [graduate_admission.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/ph/graduate_admission.pdf)
- The policy's stated purpose is to set standards for admitting graduate applicants, based on "a holistic review of the applicant's credentials." — [graduate_admission.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/ph/graduate_admission.pdf)
- The policy also states standardized test scores are required for all PhD programs offered in English (this appears to be a general/university-wide statement, which conflicts with program-specific pages — see GRE section below). — [graduate_admission.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/ph/graduate_admission.pdf)
- A separate document, "Terms of Reference — University Conditional Admission Committee," states: "This Standing Committee is advisory to the Provost and shall evaluate all conditional admission cases," and lists "Not attaining the required score on a standardized test" as a case type the committee reviews. — [terms_of_reference-_university_conditional_admission_committee.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/html_static/terms_of_reference-_university_conditional_admission_committee.pdf)
- The same document states: "Such a petition is normally limited to applicants seeking a master's level admission," and "Conditional admission for PhD may only be considered under exceptional circumstances." Committee proceedings are confidential. — [terms_of_reference-_university_conditional_admission_committee.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/html_static/terms_of_reference-_university_conditional_admission_committee.pdf)
- HBKU's graduate readmission policy separately describes "Conditional admission with specific conditions that must be attained prior to full admission" — this applies to readmission after a prior separation from the university, not first-time applicants. — [graduate_readmission.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/ph/graduate_readmission.pdf)
- English-proficiency shortfalls appear to be handled via a separate exemption mechanism rather than the conditional-admission committee: HBKU's CIS undergraduate admission policy states applicants "may apply for exemption from the English proficiency requirements," and several HBKU college admission-requirements pages point to admissions.hbku.edu.qa for the exemption process. — [undergraduate_admission-cis.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/2025-03/undergraduate_admission-cis.pdf); [CIS Admission Requirements](https://hbku.edu.qa/cis-admission-requirements)
- HBKU's undergraduate admission policy (separate from the graduate PhD context, but the only place conditions are itemized) gives examples of conditions that can be imposed: "interviews, retaking a standardized test, and providing additional information regarding significant changes in the academic record." — [undergraduate policy excerpt via search, source: hbku.edu.qa admission policy pages]
- No deadline for clearing a conditional-admission condition (e.g., a missing test score) was found in any HBKU document. The committee's terms of reference do not specify a timeline, and no condition-specific consequence-for-non-compliance language was found.

### Inferences
- Because PhD conditional admission is explicitly restricted to "exceptional circumstances" (per the Conditional Admission Committee's terms of reference), a PhD applicant should NOT assume a missing GRE or IELTS/TOEFL score will routinely be accommodated via conditional admission the way it might be for a master's applicant. This is a committee-level exception, not a standard/advertised pathway.
- The fact that "not attaining the required score on a standardized test" is explicitly named as a reviewable case type suggests GRE (a standardized test) is the kind of gap conditional admission COULD in principle address for a PhD applicant — but only under the "exceptional circumstances" bar, with Provost pre-approval, and with no published deadline or waiver process the applicant can rely on in advance.
- English-proficiency (IELTS/TOEFL) gaps seem to run through a distinct "exemption" track, not the conditional-admission committee — meaning the two issues (GRE vs. English score) may need to be raised with HBKU admissions as separate questions, not assumed to be handled identically.

### Gaps

- Could not find any document stating what the applicant must affirmatively do, or by what date, to "clear" a conditional-admission condition tied to a missing GRE/IELTS/TOEFL score, nor what happens if they fail to do so (e.g., rescinded admission vs. extended conditional period).
- Could not independently verify exact document wording by opening the PDFs directly (network-blocked); quotes above are as reported by the search tool's content extraction, not my own direct read.

## Does HBKU require GRE with zero exceptions for the Sustainable Energy PhD, or is it ever waived/deferred?

### Takeaway
GRE appears genuinely required (not optional) specifically for the Sustainable Energy PhD/MS, based on multiple HBKU program-level sources — this contrasts with at least one other CSE PhD (Computer Science and Engineering) where GRE/GMAT is explicitly "optional." No HBKU source found states any waiver/deferral specifically for Sustainable Energy based on strong GPA, work experience, or English-medium education — but see the conditional-admission exceptional-circumstances pathway above, which is the only documented (if narrow and discretionary) route.

### Cited Findings
- HBKU's CSE MS/PhD Sustainable Energy factsheet states: "applicants to the Master of Science and PhD Programs in Sustainable Energy must submit a GRE General [Test] score," and lists institutional codes GRE: 7551, GMAT: H6S, TOEFL: 4981 (IELTS needs no code). — [cse-ms-sustainable-energy-factsheet.pdf](https://hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/program-files/cse-ms-sustainable-energy-factsheet.pdf)
- A separate HBKU CSE admissions table reportedly marks the PhD in Sustainable Energy as "Required" for GRE/GMAT (vs. "Optional" for some other CSE PhDs, e.g., Computer Science and Engineering, and Logistics and Supply Chain Management). — [admission requirements](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/cse/admission-requirements)
- By contrast, the PhD in Computer Science and Engineering factsheet states submitting a GRE/GMAT score "at the time of application is optional," confirming GRE requirement is program-specific at HBKU/CSE, not uniform. — [cse-phd-computer-science-engineering-factsheet.pdf](https://hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/program-files/cse-phd-computer-science-engineering-factsheet.pdf)
- The PhD in Sustainable Energy program page itself is reported to say applicants are "required to submit a GRE General test score at the time of application, where applicable under CSE admissions requirements" — the "where applicable" qualifier is on the program page but the college factsheet drops this qualifier and states the requirement plainly. — [PhD in Sustainable Energy](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/en/cse/phd-sustainable-energy) / [Program Mission Statement](https://hbku.edu.qa/en/node/4122)
- The college's required-documents list is reported to mention "standardized test results, where required, including GRE, GMAT, TOEFL, or IELTS scores" — ambiguous as to whether "where required" means GRE can be excused for this specific program. — [admission requirements](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/cse/admission-requirements)
- HBKU's general graduate admission policy states standardized test scores "are required for all PhD programs offered in English" (university-wide rule, consistent with Sustainable Energy being a required-GRE program, but in apparent tension with the Computer Science and Engineering PhD's "optional" GRE/GMAT). — [graduate_admission.pdf](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/ph/graduate_admission.pdf)
- No HBKU source found describes a GRE waiver/exception for Sustainable Energy PhD applicants based on strong GPA, prior research/work experience, or an English-medium prior degree.
- Minimum GPA cited for the program: 3.0/4.0 (college factsheet). — [cse-ms-sustainable-energy-factsheet.pdf](https://hbku.edu.qa/sites/default/files/program-files/cse-ms-sustainable-energy-factsheet.pdf)
- English proficiency minimums cited (university/college-wide, not Sustainable-Energy-specific page confirmed): IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL 79, "unless exempted," with scores no more than two years old. — [CHSS admission requirements](https://hbku.edu.qa/en/node/17400)

### Inferences
- The "where applicable" wording on the PhD program page, versus the flat requirement on the college factsheet and the admissions table marking it "Required," suggests the factsheet/table is the more authoritative, current statement for this specific program: GRE should be treated as required with no advertised waiver, not as optional.
- The only documented path that could conceivably excuse a missing GRE score for a PhD applicant is the Conditional Admission Committee's "exceptional circumstances" discretion (see above) — this is not a stated waiver policy, has no published criteria, and is explicitly described as atypical for PhD level. An applicant should not plan around it.
- The contrast with the Computer Science & Engineering PhD (GRE optional) indicates GRE policy varies by specific program within CSE, so any waiver claim must be verified against the Sustainable Energy program specifically, not CSE/HBKU generally.

### Gaps
- Could not find a definitive, single current official page that resolves the "where applicable" qualifier — i.e., under what circumstance (if any) GRE would NOT apply to a Sustainable Energy PhD applicant. This needs direct confirmation from admissions.cse.sus@hbku.edu.qa.
- Could not confirm whether the GRE requirement differs for international vs. Qatari/resident/funded applicants for this specific program.
- Could not directly view the full CSE admission-requirements table (search snippets only); could not independently confirm the "Required" designation's current accuracy or last-updated date.

## What is the actual, current application deadline for the PhD in Sustainable Energy, and how does this reconcile with the "1 Feb 2027" vs. "March 1" conflict?

### Takeaway
No official HBKU source for a 2027/2028 intake deadline was directly fetched (network-blocked); third-party sources (a job-board posting and scholarship aggregators) give 1 February 2027 (international) and 15 March 2027 (Qatari/resident, funded), which align with the pattern of HBKU's own confirmed 2026-cycle dates (1 Feb 2026 international / 15 Mar 2026 resident-funded / 3 May 2026 late). The "March 1" figure from the 2018-dated policy PDF could not be corroborated as an application deadline at all — 2018-era HBKU press coverage instead cites 1 April 2018 as the deadline, with one undated Gulf Times article mentioning "March 1" only for applicants living abroad (deadline for local/resident applicants was April 1). This suggests the 2018 "March 1" figure may be outdated, applicant-category-specific, or misreported by the third-party source citing it — it should NOT be treated as the current deadline.

### Cited Findings

## Must GRE/IELTS/TOEFL scores be in hand at time of application, or can pending/unofficial scores be submitted with official ones to follow later?

### Takeaway
No HBKU source was found that explicitly states a pending/unofficial-score-now, official-later policy for GRE or English tests. Available HBKU language instead describes applicants submitting copies of test scores with the online application while official copies are sent directly to HBKU "where required" — this is ambiguous as to timing and does not confirm whether an applicant can apply before sitting the test/receiving results.

### Cited Findings
- An HBKU admissions page is reported to state: "Official copies (where required) must be sent directly to HBKU. ... Applicants should also submit copies of their test scores with the online application." This implies the applicant already has scores (official or self-reported copies) to submit alongside the application, not that scores can be entirely pending. — [admission requirements](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/cse/admission-requirements) (exact page not fully confirmed by direct fetch)
- The PhD Sustainable Energy program page is reported to require "a GRE General test score at the time of application, where applicable" — the phrase "at the time of application" argues against a pending/later-submission allowance for GRE specifically. — [PhD in Sustainable Energy](https://www.hbku.edu.qa/en/cse/phd-sustainable-energy)
- HBKU's CHSS admission-requirements page states IELTS/TOEFL scores "must be no more than two years old," implying scores must already exist, but does not address whether they must be submitted by the application deadline itself or can follow shortly after. — [CHSS Admission Requirements](https://hbku.edu.qa/en/node/17400)
- No HBKU FAQ, policy, or program page found explicitly permits "unofficial now, official later" submission, in contrast to common practice at some other universities (where this is sometimes called a "conditional" or "provisional" document submission allowance).

### Inferences
- HBKU's wording ("at the time of application," scores submitted "with the online application") leans toward requiring scores in hand at application time, not after — but this is an inference from ambiguous phrasing, not a directly confirmed "no pending scores allowed" statement.
- Given the explicit, narrow nature of the Conditional Admission Committee pathway (PhD = "exceptional circumstances" only), an applicant should not assume submitting an application without GRE/English scores and hoping to supply them later is a normal, low-risk option for this program — it would likely have to go through that discretionary, Provost-approved exception process, with no guaranteed outcome and no published timeline.

### Gaps
- Could not find an explicit HBKU statement on whether unofficial/self-reported/pending test scores are acceptable at initial application with official scores to follow, versus scores being mandatory in full at submission.
- Could not confirm via direct fetch of the CSE admission-requirements page or the Sustainable Energy program page (network-blocked), so phrasing above should be verified by the applicant directly on hbku.edu.qa or by emailing admissions.cse.sus@hbku.edu.qa before applying.
- No information found on whether HBKU's online application portal technically allows submission to proceed without test scores attached (a practical/systems question separate from written policy).

## Overall confidence summary for the applicant

- **High confidence (multiple independent HBKU-sourced snippets, consistent across searches):** GRE is treated as "Required" (not optional) specifically for the Sustainable Energy MS/PhD program, per the college factsheet and admissions table — this is a program-specific designation that differs from at least one other CSE PhD (Computer Science and Engineering, where it's optional).
- **Medium confidence:** Conditional admission exists as a mechanism, can in principle cover a missing standardized-test score, but is explicitly restricted for PhD applicants to "exceptional circumstances," requires Provost pre-approval, and has no published criteria, deadline, or guaranteed availability — this is not a safety net to plan around.
- **Low confidence / unconfirmed:** The exact 2027/2028 PhD application deadline (1 Feb 2027 is the best available figure, sourced only via aggregators, not confirmed directly on hbku.edu.qa in this session).
- **Unresolved / likely miscategorized:** The "March 1, 2018" deadline figure the user previously found does not match 2018-era press coverage (which says April 1); it may refer to a different applicant category (e.g., "applicants living abroad," or an undergraduate intake) rather than being in genuine conflict with the 1 Feb 2027 figure for the PhD program.
- **Not found anywhere:** Any explicit HBKU statement allowing pending/unofficial GRE or English scores at application with official scores to follow, or any stated GRE waiver criteria (GPA, experience, English-medium degree) for this specific program.
